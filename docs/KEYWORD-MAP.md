Every number here came from Semrush on 3 September 2026, US and AU databases.
Nothing is estimated or remembered.

**KD** = keyword difficulty, 0–100. Under 30 is winnable for a site with no
authority yet. Over 60 is a two-year project and not where a relaunch starts.

## 6. What the SERPs actually look like — and why it changes the plan

Difficulty scores say how hard a term is. They do not say **what shape of page
Google has decided to rank for it**, and that turns out to matter more.

### `after hours answering service` — 2,400 vol, $62.03, KD 11

Nine of the top twelve results are US city and state landing pages:

```
2   absentanswer.com/coverage/oklahoma-answering-service/
4   mapcommunications.com/services/states/nevada/las-vegas-answering-service/
5   answerfirst.com/california/san-francisco-answering-service/
6   responsiveanswering.com/arizona/phoenix-answering-service/
7   mapcommunications.com/services/states/california/los-angeles-answering-service/
9   responsiveanswering.com/texas/houston-answering-service/
12  responsiveanswering.com/wisconsin/milwaukee-answering-service/
```

Only position 1 is a topical service page. The difficulty is 11 *because* the
competition is thin programmatic geo pages — but Google is clearly reading
local intent into this query.

**What that means here.** We do not have US locations, and inventing city pages
for offices that do not exist is precisely the kind of thing this site refuses
to do. The realistic target is position 1's slot — a genuinely better topical
page — not the geo block beneath it. Still worth having. No longer "the single
highest priority on the site", which was an overstatement made before this SERP
was looked at.

### The conclusion

**Google has decided the head terms are research queries, not purchase
queries.** This site is made entirely of service pages. A service page entered
against "best outsourcing companies for 2026" does not lose because it is
worse — it loses because it is answering a different question.

That splits the target list in two:

| Shape of SERP | Terms | What wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Service pages rank** | virtual receptionist, virtual assistant services, kyc outsourcing, order taking services, email support outsourcing, data entry outsourcing, meta ads agency | The pages we already have. Titles now match. Nothing more needed. |
| **Guides and listicles rank** | customer support outsourcing, back office outsourcing, call center outsourcing, outsourced customer service | A service page cannot win these. Two ways in, neither of them a code change. |

**The two ways in, stated plainly:**

1. **Write guides.** A `/guides` or `/resources` section — genuinely useful
   long-form pieces answering the research question. This is a structural
   addition to the site and a real content commitment, not a metadata tweak.
2. **Get listed in the listicles that already rank.** Clutch, Hugo's round-ups,
   business.com. That is outreach and profile work, not something buildable.

Neither was in scope for this pass. Both are decisions rather than tasks.

## 7. Two rules applied throughout

1. **Search vocabulary goes in titles, H1s and metadata. The body keeps its
   voice.** The site's copy is its main asset — it does not read like every
   other BPO site, and that is why it converts. Keywords are placed where
   crawlers weight them, not sprayed through prose.

2. **No page claims a service the company does not run.** The `/operations`
   pages describe pooled staffed desks, not dedicated 1:1 assistants. Where a
   keyword implies otherwise — `hire virtual assistant` does — the page names
   the difference rather than pretending.
